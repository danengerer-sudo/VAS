WHERE DO GUN RIGHTS COME FROM?
(read at a relaxed pace — pause one beat at each blank line)

Ask most people where gun rights come from, and you'll get the same answer: the Second Amendment.

It's the wrong answer.

If your right to own a gun comes from a document, then it can be taken away by a document.
Amend it, reinterpret it, repeal it... and the "right" is gone.

But a right that only exists because someone wrote it down — and only for as long as they let it stand — isn't a right at all.
It's a permission slip. Like a hall pass, but for owning things.

So try a different question.
Where does your right to own a television come from?

There's no amendment for it. No one has ever asked.
Not even your mother-in-law — and she has opinions about everything.

You earned the money, you traded it for the TV, and now it's yours.
You don't need a document to own it, because owning it doesn't hurt anyone.

That's the whole idea.
You own yourself, and you own what you peacefully acquire.

A rifle locked in a safe hurts no one — any more than a TV on a wall does.

Now picture actually banning one.
Someone has to come to your door.
Someone has to take it — by force if you refuse — and cage you if you resist.

Notice the TV never kicks anyone's door in.

Owning the object harmed no one.
The ban is the part that requires aggression.

None of this means anything goes.
Using a gun to rob or threaten someone is aggression, and the victim has every right to defend themselves and seek justice.

But the wrong is the robbery — not the ownership.
The same is true of a car, a kitchen knife, or a hammer.

You punish the act, not the object.
Nobody's arresting the hammer.

So where do gun rights come from?
Not from the Second Amendment.

They come from the same place as your right to own a TV, a book, or a hammer:
from the simple fact that peacefully owning something violates no one.

And the document people point to? It's the same piece of paper politicians wave around to excuse taking your money by force.
Paper can't give anyone the right to rob you. And it can't give you your rights, either.

And the real question was never "Why should you be allowed to own a gun?"
It's this:
Who would have to use force to stop you?

(beat)

And if you don't like it... you can kiss my ass.
