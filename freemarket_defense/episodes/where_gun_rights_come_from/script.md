# Where Do Gun Rights Come From? — script v2 (~430 words, ~2:50–3:00)

## 1. Hook (0:00–0:15)
Ask most people where gun rights come from, and you'll get the same answer: the Second Amendment.
It's the wrong answer.

*Shot: slow push toward a white classical government building (parliament_dome) at golden hour; a contract asset floats in front labelled "2nd Amendment".*

## 2. The problem with paper (0:15–0:40)
If your right to own a gun comes from a document, then it can be taken away by a document.
Amend it, reinterpret it, repeal it, and the "right" is gone.
But a right that exists only because someone wrote it down, and only for as long as they let it stand, isn't a right at all. It's a permission slip. Like a hall pass, but for owning things.

*Shot: drift across a desk: document, pen, gavel. A second document slides over the first. GAG: cut to a figure holding up a giant laminated "HALL PASS" (accent colour).*

## 3. The TV (0:40–1:10)
So try a different question. Where does your right to own a television come from?
There's no amendment for it. No one has ever asked. Not even your mother-in-law, and she has opinions about everything.
You earned the money, you traded it for the TV, and now it's yours.
You don't need a document to own it, because owning it doesn't hurt anyone.

*Shot: a person carries a boxed TV out of a storefront; cut to a living room (furniture kit), TV on a stand, a family on the sofa.*

## 4. The principle (1:10–1:45)
That's the whole idea. You own yourself, and you own what you peacefully acquire.
A rifle locked in a safe hurts no one, any more than a TV on a wall does.
Now picture actually banning one. Someone has to come to your door. Someone has to take it, by force if you refuse, and cage you if you resist.
Notice the TV never kicks anyone's door in.
Owning the object harmed no one. The ban is the part that requires aggression.

*Shot: dolly up a suburban street to a house; a small group of figures in accent-coloured gear walks toward the front door; hold on the door. GAG: cut to the TV sitting innocently on its stand.*

## 5. Crime is an act, not an object (1:45–2:10)
None of this means anything goes. Using a gun to rob or threaten someone is aggression, and the victim has every right to defend themselves and seek justice.
But the wrong is the robbery, not the ownership. The same is true of a car, a kitchen knife, or a hammer.
You punish the act, not the object. Nobody's arresting the hammer.

*Shot: slow orbit around a table: rifle, car keys, kitchen knife, hammer, each with a small label. GAG: the hammer in a tiny jail cell with a "NOT GUILTY" placard.*

## 6. Close (2:10–2:45)
So where do gun rights come from? Not from the Second Amendment.
They come from the same place as your right to own a TV, a book, or a hammer: from the simple fact that peacefully owning something violates no one.
And the document people point to? It's the same piece of paper politicians wave around to excuse taking your money by force.
Paper can't give anyone the right to rob you. And it can't give you your rights, either.
And the real question was never "Why should you be allowed to own a gun?"
It's this: who would have to use force to stop you?

And if you don't like it, you can kiss my—
